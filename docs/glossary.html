Basics

Target site#

A landing site or base candidate on Mars or the Moon for which Earth analogs are sought: 14 on Mars, 13 at the lunar south pole, from the Moon/Mars team's site list. Each target is a box with values for the features.

Earth cell#

A 0.05° × 0.05° grid cell on Earth, about 5.5 × 5.5 km at the equator and narrower towards the poles. Earth has 8.9 million such land cells, each with averages over 2003–2024.

Ranked cells#

The cells that enter the ranking: only vegetation-free land, without masked areas, with enough data (minimum coverage). About 840,000 of the 8.9 million land cells per target.

Vegetation-free#

A cell counts as vegetation-free if its yearly NDVI peak is below 0.15 or MODIS classes at least 50 % of it as ‘barren’. The yearly peak rather than the mean, so bright desert soil (Atacama, NDVI 0.12) is not taken for vegetation.

Masked areas#

Not ranked: cells over 50 % water; ice sheets and glaciers (permanent snow that stays below 280 K even in the warmest month); forest, cropland and settlements as the main land cover (MODIS); everything that is not vegetation-free.

Rank on Earth#

Place of a cell among all ranked cells, sorted by the overall score. Rank 1 is the best-matching cell on Earth. ‘#105 of 837,533’ means 104 cells match better and the cell is in the best 0.013 %. Ranks jump in the lists because cells next to a better site are skipped (Top 20).

Top 20 (at least 200 km apart)#

The 20 best sites per target. So that 20 neighbouring cells of one area do not fill the list, each site must be at least 200 km from every better one. That is why site no. 4 in the list is often cell rank 15 or 30.

Best site per continent#

For each target, the best cell on every continent (continents as in Natural Earth), sorted by rank on Earth. Shows how far the best alternative outside the leading region falls behind. Summaries give the median over all targets of one body per continent: ‘Mars: South America 105’ means that for a typical Mars target the best South American site is rank 105.

Exclude China#

Option for missions where fieldwork in China is not possible. Removes all cells inside the People's Republic of China's de-facto borders (Natural Earth, so including disputed areas China administers such as Aksai Chin) and Hong Kong and Macau, then re-ranks the list. Taiwan is not removed. The scores themselves do not change.

Known analog / new#

‘Known’: the site lies within the radius of an established analog site from the literature (list under known analogs). ‘New’: no known site nearby. The method does not see this list while scoring; it is only used for the label.

Positive control#

A check that the method finds sensible places: established analogs (Qaidam Basin, Atacama, Altiplano, Axel Heiberg, Haughton crater) appear in the lists although the method does not know them. The number in brackets is the count of hits within that site's radius.

Similarity map#

The coloured world map in the web app: every ranked cell coloured by its rank, area-weighted. Only the best 35 % are shown (from the 65th percentile), brighter is more similar. The map uses the coarser 0.25° grid; the lists use the fine 0.05° grid.

Country and disputed areas#

Country names from Natural Earth 1:50m (public domain), only for labels and the Exclude China filter. Natural Earth draws de-facto borders; disputed areas are marked, e.g. ‘Aksai Chin (disputed; administered by China)’.

Known analogs#

Established analog sites from the literature. A site within the radius counts as ‘known’.

Scoring

Feature#

A measurable quantity that target and Earth cell share, e.g. slope, albedo or precipitation (list under features). Each feature belongs to a Dimension and is compared with one of three methods (value against value, rank against rank, beyond Earth's range).

Dimension#

A group of features describing one property, e.g. ‘Terrain’ from median and steep slope and relief. A dimension's score is the weighted mean of its match scores. Nine dimensions: terrain, surface material, temperature, dryness, atmosphere, dust & wind, ice & water, vegetation, sunlight.

Match score#

How well an Earth cell matches the target for one feature, from 0 to 1; 1 means as close as Earth gets. How it is computed depends on the method (value against value, rank against rank, beyond Earth's range).

Value against value#

For the same quantity measured the same way (terrain: slope and relief from elevation models with the same code). Match = 1 − |Earth value − target value| / span, where span is the larger distance of the target value to Earth's 2nd or 98th percentile.

Rank against rank#

For quantities measured with different instruments on target and Earth (albedo, thermal inertia). Not the value is compared but the standing on its own body: a site brighter than 41 % of Mars matches Earth land brighter than 41 % of Earth's bare land. See body percentile.

Beyond Earth's range#

For values Earth never reaches: vacuum, 6 hPa air pressure, −170 °C, no precipitation, the Sun just above the horizon. Match = share of Earth's land that is further from the target: ‘lower than 91 % of Earth's land’ gives 0.91. The closer to the target value, the better.

Body percentile#

Share of a body's surface with a lower value, area-weighted. Example: Jezero has a thermal inertia of 304 TIU, more than 89 % of Mars's surface (percentile 0.89). On Earth the reference is bare, ice-free land (common/body_percentile.py).

Reliability grade A / B / C#

Grade for every target value and Earth feature: A direct measurement, B coarse or derived, C rough estimate. It sets the weight: A = 1, B = 0.7, C = 0.4, multiplied for target and Earth. A feature graded C on the target and C on Earth counts only 0.16.

Weight#

How much a feature counts within its Dimension: reliability of the target value × reliability of the Earth feature (see reliability grade). In the web app it is shown when hovering a match bar.

Dimension confidence#

The mean weight of a dimension's available features. Dimensions built from uncertain values (e.g. only grade C) count correspondingly less in the overall score.

Overall score#

A number from 0 to 1 per cell, used only for sorting: weighted mean of the dimension scores, weight = the profile's dimension weight × confidence. It sets the rank on Earth. The profile of dimensions says more than the number.

Target profile (weights)#

Each target belongs to one of three profiles; the profile sets how much each Dimension counts in the overall score (0 = not at all). Table below.

Share of the profile's weight for which the Earth cell has values; 100 % means data for every weighted dimension.

Minimum coverage 80 %#

A cell only enters the ranking if it covers at least 80% of the weight of the dimensions the target has values for. Otherwise cells with data gaps would rise, because missing dimensions would simply be left out.

Strongest / weakest dimension#

The Dimension with the highest or lowest score at this site: where the analogy holds best (‘Strongest’) and where it breaks (‘Weakest’).

Environment classes

Environment class#

Every Earth cell is put into one of ten landscape types by fixed rules. Classes are tested in a fixed order (as listed below); the first whose rules all hold wins. A rule starting with ‘or’ is an alternative to the line above. The class does not enter the overall score; it describes the site.

Ice sheet & glacier#

Snow or ice all year.
Link to Mars/Moon: Mars polar caps, ice in lunar cold traps (as an ice process, not as terrain).
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • permanent snow in >= 80 % of months
  • or MODIS snow/ice >= 60 %
  • and warmest month < 280 K

Salt flat / evaporite#

Very flat, bright, vegetation-free: dried-out salt lakes.
Link to Mars/Moon: Chloride and sulfate deposits on Mars (e.g. Meridiani, Terra Sirenum).
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • snow-free albedo > 0.35
  • flat: >= 95 % of area below 5 deg
  • relief < 100 m
  • elevation spread < 5 m
  • warmest month > 280 K (not snow)
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Dune field / sand sea#

Vegetation-free wind-blown sand: low thermal inertia, medium albedo, dry.
Link to Mars/Moon: Martian dunes and wind processes (e.g. Bagnold dunes in Gale crater).
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • MODIS 'barren' >= 80 %
  • albedo 0.25 to 0.45
  • loose ground: apparent thermal inertia < 0.026
  • precipitation < 150 mm/yr
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Dark bare rock (basalt, lava)#

Snow-free albedo below 0.15 without vegetation: basalt, lava fields, volcanic ash.
Link to Mars/Moon: Basaltic plains on Mars and lunar maria; lava-tube regions.
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • snow-free albedo < 0.15
  • water < 30 %
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Ice-free cold polar desert#

Cold, dry, hardly any vegetation, snow-free in summer.
Link to Mars/Moon: Martian mid-latitudes with ground ice; regolith of the lunar polar regions.
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • mean surface temperature < 263 K
  • not permanent snow (< 80 % of months)
  • precipitation < 400 mm/yr
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Arid high plateau#

Above 3000 m, dry and barren: low pressure, large day-night range, strong UV.
Link to Mars/Moon: Mars: thin air, strong temperature swings, UV (e.g. Altiplano, Chang Tang).
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • elevation > 3000 m
  • precipitation < 400 mm/yr
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Hyperarid desert#

Below 50 mm of precipitation a year, practically vegetation-free.
Link to Mars/Moon: Dryness and oxidation of Martian soil; the limit of life (e.g. Atacama core).
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • precipitation < 50 mm/yr
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Barren steep terrain#

Mean slope above 15° without notable vegetation.
Link to Mars/Moon: Crater rims, canyon walls (Valles Marineris), crater rims at the lunar south pole.
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • mean slope > 15 deg
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Arid desert / semi-desert#

Below 250 mm of precipitation and sparse vegetation, without the properties above.
Link to Mars/Moon: General desert analog for instrument tests and mobility.
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • precipitation < 250 mm/yr
  • vegetation-free: NDVI yearly peak < 0.15
  • or MODIS land cover 'barren' >= 50 %

Bare, but no class matched#

Among the ranked (vegetation-free) sites: bare ground that fits none of the classes above, mostly because it is wetter (e.g. over 400 mm of precipitation) or milder. Elsewhere: vegetated, wet or mostly water.
Rules (all must hold; ‘or’ = alternative to the line above):

  • none of the rules above applies (vegetated, wet or icy)

Flags

Flag#

Properties shown in addition to the class; a site can have several. They do not enter the overall score.

Dust source#

Dust emission in the top 5 % of land areas (MERRA-2).

Frequent dust storms#

At least 10 dust-storm days a year (dust AOD above 1).

Frequent gales#

At least 5 gale days a year (wind above 17 m/s).

Calm#

Little wind and no storms (mean wind below 3 m/s): stable conditions as on the Moon.

Freeze-thaw#

At least 3 months a year with freeze-thaw cycles.

Extreme cold#

Coldest month below 220 K (−53 °C) at night.

Low pressure#

Air pressure below 700 hPa (roughly above 3000 m).

Dark surface#

Snow-free albedo below 0.15 on vegetation-free ground (basalt, lava, ash).

Landing-friendly#

At least 80 % of the area below 5° slope, 90th-percentile slope below 8°.

Polar light#

Inside the polar circle: polar day and night, low sun as at the lunar south pole.

Features

Features at a glance#

The quantities that make target and Earth comparable. For each: what it measures, where Earth and target values come from, which method compares them. Units: K kelvin (0 °C = 273.15 K), hPa hectopascal, TIU = J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½.

Slope, median#

Typical steepness of the ground in the target box or Earth cell, from the elevation model coarsened to the target's pixel size first (100 m Moon, 463 m Mars). Earth: Copernicus GLO-90; Mars: MOLA; Moon: LOLA; the same code everywhere (common/terrain.py).
Dimension: Terrain · Method: value against value · Earth reliability: A

Slope, 90th percentile#

How steep the steepest 10 % of the area are: a measure of obstacles for landing and driving. Computed like the median.
Dimension: Terrain · Method: value against value · Earth reliability: A

Relief (P95−P5, 5 km)#

Elevation span between the 5th and 95th percentile within 5 km windows: how hilly or rugged the area is.
Dimension: Terrain · Method: value against value · Earth reliability: A

Albedo#

Share of sunlight reflected (0 black, 1 white). Earth: MODIS, snow-free; Mars: TES Lambert albedo. Compared by rank because the instruments measure different wavelengths.
Dimension: Surface material · Method: rank against rank · Earth reliability: A

Thermal inertia#

How slowly a surface changes temperature: dust and sand low, rock high. Mars: TES, night-time (TIU). Earth: apparent thermal inertia ATI = (1 − albedo) / day-night temperature range (1/K). Compared by rank.
Dimension: Surface material · Method: rank against rank · Earth reliability: B

Dust opacity#

How much airborne dust dims the light (optical depth, AOD). Earth: MERRA-2. No Mars target value yet.
Dimension: Dust & wind · Method: rank against rank · Earth reliability: A

Dust storm days#

Days per year with dust AOD above 1.0 (MERRA-2, daily values 2003–2024).
Dimension: Dust & wind · Method: rank against rank · Earth reliability: B

Mean wind#

Mean surface wind speed (MERRA-2), m/s. Moon: 0 (no wind).
Dimension: Dust & wind · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Mean surface temperature#

Mean ground temperature from four MODIS overpasses per day, 2003–2024 (K). Moon: Diviner; Mars: estimate (grade C).
Dimension: Temperature · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Coldest month#

Mean night-time ground temperature in the coldest month (MODIS, K).
Dimension: Temperature · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Day-night range#

Difference between the warmest and coldest of the four daily MODIS measurements, averaged (K). Mars: estimate (grade C).
Dimension: Temperature · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Surface pressure#

Surface air pressure (MERRA-2, hPa). Mars: rough estimate from MOLA elevation (6.1 hPa · e^(−height/11.1 km), grade C); Moon: vacuum.
Dimension: Atmosphere · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Humidity#

Specific humidity at 2 m (MERRA-2, g of water vapour per kg of air).
Dimension: Atmosphere · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Precipitation#

Yearly precipitation (MERRA-2 PRECTOTCORR, mm/yr). Mars and Moon: 0.
Dimension: Dryness · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Vegetation (NDVI)#

How green an area is (MODIS, yearly mean): below 0.1 bare, above 0.3 clearly vegetated. Mars and Moon: 0.
Dimension: Vegetation · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Ground ice#

Moon: share of the area where water ice is stable within 1 m depth (Diviner). Earth: rough proxy from mean yearly temperature ((272.15 K − T) / 7, limited to 0–1, grade C). Mars: estimate.
Dimension: Ice & water · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: C

Highest sun elevation#

Highest elevation of the Sun above the horizon over the year: 90° − |latitude| + axial tilt (Earth 23.44°, Mars 25.19°, Moon 1.54°). Only a few degrees at the lunar south pole, never below 23.4° on Earth.
Dimension: Sunlight · Method: beyond Earth's range · Earth reliability: A

Target sites

Target box#

Each target is a square around the site list's coordinate: 10 × 10 km on Mars, 15 × 15 km on the Moon. Target values are medians (or statistics) over this square.

Artemis regions and crater rims#

NASA's Artemis III candidate regions are sunlit areas on crater rims. The site list often gives the crater centre, which lies in permanent shadow. So the target box is placed on the best-lit square nearby (mean LOLA sun visibility): within 12 km, for crater regions (Haworth, Nobile, de Gerlache, Faustini, Slater) within crater radius + 10 km.

Coordinate quality#

‘verified’: exact published coordinate (e.g. Curiosity's Bradbury Landing). ‘approx.’: approximate (rounded or region centre). ‘unverified’: uncertain source. Shown in the web app's target list.

Permanently shadowed region (PSR)#

Craters at the lunar poles whose floors never see the Sun (below 110 K, partly below 40 K), where water ice is stable. Targets: Shackleton crater, Cabeus. No place on Earth is that cold.

Data

MODIS (NASA)#

Instrument on NASA's Terra and Aqua satellites. Used: surface temperature at four times of day (MOD11C3/MYD11C3), albedo incl. snow-free (MCD43C3), land cover (MCD12C1), vegetation index NDVI (MOD13C2), snow (MOD10CM). 0.05°, 2003–2024.

MERRA-2 (NASA)#

NASA atmospheric reanalysis (about 55 km grid): dust optical depth, dust emission, dust-storm and gale days, wind, pressure, humidity, precipitation (PRECTOTCORR). Regional, not local: mountains and valleys in one grid cell share a value.

Copernicus DEM GLO-90 (ESA)#

Global elevation model with 90 m pixels; gives slope and relief of the Earth cells, coarsened to 100 m or 463 m like the targets.

MOLA (NASA, Mars)#

Mars Global Surveyor's laser altimeter. 463 m mosaic (USGS) for the Mars targets' terrain; the app's Mars map uses the MEGDR product at 4 pixels per degree (NASA PDS).

LOLA (NASA, Moon)#

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's laser altimeter. Polar elevation model LDEM_80S_80M (80 m) for the Moon targets' terrain and the Moon map; sun visibility for placing the Artemis regions.

TES (NASA, Mars)#

Mars Global Surveyor's Thermal Emission Spectrometer: night-time thermal inertia (Putzig & Mellon 2007, NASA PDS, 3 km) and Lambert albedo (USGS mosaic, 7.4 km).

Diviner (NASA, Moon)#

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's radiometer. Polar Resource Product: surface temperatures and the depth at which water ice is stable, as a 240 m grid for 85–90° S (prepared by the Moon/Mars team).

Natural Earth#

Public-domain country borders and continents (1:50m, 1:110m). Only labels, the Exclude China filter and the base map; no measurements.
