    How it works

    The idea

    Every target site on the Moon or Mars gets a fingerprint: terrain, surface material, temperature, dryness, atmosphere, dust, ice and sunlight. Every patch of Earth's land gets the same fingerprint from NASA and ESA data. The finder ranks Earth by how closely each patch matches, and shows for each match where the analogy holds and where it breaks.

    Comparing different worlds

    • Same quantity, same method (terrain): value against value. Slope and relief are computed with the same code on all three bodies, at the target's resolution (100 m Moon, 463 m Mars).
    • Different instruments (thermal inertia, albedo): rank against rank. A site that is rockier than 41 % of Mars is matched with Earth land that is rockier than 41 % of Earth's bare ground.
    • Out of Earth's reach (vacuum, 6 hPa, −170 °C): the closer Earth gets, the better. No place on Earth reaches these values.
    • Every value carries a reliability grade (A direct measurement, B coarse or proxy, C rough estimate) that sets its weight.

    Data

    • Earth, 2003–2024: NASA MODIS (surface temperature, albedo, vegetation, snow, land cover), NASA MERRA-2 (dust, dust storms, wind, pressure, precipitation), ESA Copernicus DEM (terrain, 90 m).
    • Mars: MOLA elevation (terrain), TES thermal inertia (NASA PDS), TES albedo (USGS mosaic of NASA TES data).
    • Moon: LOLA polar elevation (terrain), Diviner polar resource product (temperature, ice stability) (NASA PDS).
    • Country names: Natural Earth (de-facto borders; disputed areas are marked).
    • ‘Exclude China’ removes every 5 km cell inside China's de-facto borders (with Hong Kong and Macau) and re-ranks the list, for missions where fieldwork there is not possible. The match scores themselves do not change.

    Limits

    • No place on Earth has the Moon's vacuum, Mars's thin CO₂ air, their low gravity or their radiation.
    • Target sites are 10 × 10 km (Mars) and 15 × 15 km (Moon) boxes around published coordinates, many of which are approximate.
    • Mars temperature, day-night range, pressure and ground ice are rough estimates (grade C, low weight); Mars dust (TES) and ground ice (Odyssey GRS) are not yet included.
    • Only vegetation-free land is ranked (NDVI yearly peak below 0.15 or MODIS ‘barren’ ≥ 50 %).
    • Lunar rim regions are placed on the best-lit 15 km square near the named crater (search radius = crater radius + 10 km); Nobile then lies outside the Diviner grid and has no temperature value.
    • Dust, wind and pressure on Earth come from a 55 km reanalysis grid: regional, not local.
